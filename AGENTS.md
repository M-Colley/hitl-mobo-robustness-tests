# Working in this repository

This file is for whoever works on the repository next, human or agent. `README.md` explains what the project is. This file explains how not to break it and where the traps are. Everything here was learned by hitting it.

## What the project claims

Bayesian optimization driven by human ratings is measured on twenty analytic landscapes with published optima, with four error processes injected at four magnitudes and two onsets. The deployed design's regret splits into search loss (the run never visited anything better) and selection loss (it visited something better and shipped the wrong one). Selection loss is zero under exact observation, so a noiseless benchmark cannot show it, and no acquisition function targets it directly. At 1σ from the first rating, the magnitude where the measured rater noise of the three human studies falls, selection is 41.8% [34, 50] of the deployed cost of error, rising to 83.1% [75, 90] at 5σ from trial 21. Pooled over all cells it is 61.8%, but that pool is a ratio of sums in which the 5σ cells carry 66% of the total, so it is close to the 5σ answer and is not the headline. No acquisition-side change tested improved the deployed design.

At 1σ from the first rating the gain of about a tenth belongs to a zero-trial ship rule: ship the visited design with the highest posterior mean less one latent SD (LCB1) after all trials. It recovers 10% [2, 18] of the deployed cost on the held-out seeds 12–16 (four processes pooled) and 14% [5, 21] on the untouched seeds 27–36 (Gaussian), but 4% [−2, 11] on seeds 7–11, at a price of 0.021–0.023 of the achievable improvement without error. In the four-process replication on seeds 27–36 (E1, analysis frozen before the runs) LCB1 recovers 8.1% [2, 15] and LCB2, the rule seeds 7–11 choose, 7.4% [1, 13]. All of this is with BoTorch's current default surrogate: with its default before 0.12 (E3, `--gp-prior legacy`, LogEI and qNEI, seeds 7–16) LCB2 recovers 2.7% [−9, 12] against 8.1% [1, 15] on the same runs, a difference within noise (−0.013 [−0.032, +0.003]) but a gain that is no longer detectable, so quote the tenth with its surrogate. A final sitting of two designs adds nothing measurable to it (+0.003 [−0.003, +0.009] held out, four processes; −0.008 [−0.017, +0.002] untouched, Gaussian). The final sitting's value lies where large error arrives mid-study: at 5σ from trial 21 a sitting of sixteen gains +0.227 [0.147, 0.321] of the achievable improvement held out (41% of the deployed cost, four processes pooled) and +0.249 [0.164, 0.339] over LCB1, which loses there, at a price of 0.033. Under Gaussian error alone its increment over LCB1 is +0.097 [0.030, 0.162] (Holm over k 0.12). Four confirmation trials cut false improvement claims from 15.7% to 0.5% (seeds 7–16).

For gross faults the largest recovery comes from a remedy that assumes nothing about the fault: refitting the surrogate on the ratings' ranks recovers 51% [36, 63] of the deployed cost (49% held out) at a price of 0.029 without error on those runs (0.024 held out, 0.027 on the main sweep), and it deserves the same caution as the cautious ship rule. Explicit models of the fault recover less: relevance pursuit 38% [24, 51] (price 0.011), per-rater offsets 32% [23, 40] of the cost of alternating raters (price 0.007), and clipping to the landscape's known range 22–44% depending on the tie rule (price zero by construction, because the clip acts only on noisy runs, never on the clean twin; its bounds use the known optimum, so it is not deployable as run).

Oracles fitted to archival-style ratings do not significantly understate the deployed loss at 1σ in any model family: a tree ensemble reports 1.02 [0.88, 1.19] of the exact objective's deployed loss at 1σ from the first rating, as a share of the achievable improvement (1.03 [0.90, 1.17] with each seed's error grid scaled to its own oracle). What a fitted oracle reports of the search loss depends on its family. The case for exact objectives rests on the fitted oracle's misspecification, on that dependence on an arbitrary family choice and on how its achievable improvement is set (the landscape ranking moves with the family and the normaliser jointly), and on the verified optima and measurable geometry of the exact landscapes.

## Two metrics, never interchangeable

`simple_regret_true` is `y_opt − best_true_so_far`, the regret of the best design visited so far. It is search loss. The post-onset per-iteration AUC of its excess (`auc_simple_regret_excess_true_postonset_per_iter`) is the time-averaged search loss behind the dose-response, power-law, descriptor, frag and acquisition-ranking results. `inference_simple_regret_true` is the regret of the design with the best observed rating, which is what a study ships. At the final trial the shipped design's excess is typically two to three times the search excess. Name the metric and its time base in every sentence that quotes a number, and compare a final deployed value only with a final search value.

The standard ship rule breaks ties at the FIRST index: it ships the earliest design with the best observed rating (`np.argmax` in the simulator, `bo_sensor_error_simulation.py` near l.4271, and in `rescore_ship_rules.py`; stable sorts in `replay_end_of_study.py` and `replay_hitl_remedies.py`). Under continuous error a tie never matters: the main sweep's 407 tied noisy runs and 336 tied clean twins are all exact repeated ratings (in a clean twin, or before a trial-21 onset) of designs with the same true value, and the gross-fault arm has none that matter either. On a capped, clipped or rounded scale the top rating is tied between designs of different true value in most runs (fixed cap 99.5%, clipping 52–77%, instrument 61%), and the earliest tied design is the worse one in 53–70% of them. There the tie rule moves deployed numbers by tens of points, so give the uniform-random tie-break value beside every affected number: the capped scale's extra cost 185% [132, 258] against 135% [92, 196], the re-anchored cap 25% against 18%, the rank refit under the cap 36% against 23%, clipping at 15% of trials spiking at 20 SD 22% against 44%. `python scripts/checks/tie_break.py rescore` computes them.

## Non-negotiables

Score a process change against the standard process, never against its own clean twin. Replicating ratings, re-rating before deploying, or changing the acquisition also changes the clean run, so excess over the arm's own twin measures it against a handicapped reference. This once made replication look as if it recovered a third of the cost when it recovers nothing. `scripts/analyse_boba_adaptations.py` implements the right estimand (`cost = ref_noisy − ref_clean`, `gain = ref_noisy − trt_noisy`, `price = trt_clean − ref_clean`). Import `paired_frame` and `summarise` from it instead of writing a second copy. When the reference is a pooled mean over several acquisitions, say so, because a strong single acquisition then looks like a remedy.

Score a procedure that spends trials on identification against the standard process AND against the zero-trial ship rules on the same runs: the rule it builds on, and the strongest zero-trial rule chosen on the selection seeds, not the one that looks best on the test seeds. The final sitting ranks its candidates by LCB1 (posterior mean less one latent SD), but LCB2 (less two) is the rule seeds 7–11 pick at 1σ from the first rating and at 5σ from trial 21. At 1σ LCB1 after 50 trials gains almost as much as the sitting of two (+0.027 against +0.030 held out), so the sitting's own contribution is +0.003 [−0.003, +0.009]. At 5σ from trial 21 under Gaussian error LCB2 gains +0.145, and the sitting's advantage over it is only +0.065 [0.010, 0.122]. `sitting_by_magnitude.py` writes `inc_lcb1_*`, `inc_lcb2_*`, `inc_pm_*` and `increment_over_chosen_rule`.

Never report a recovery ratio whose reference cost is near zero or changes sign across cells. Report the absolute difference instead. `compare_boba_arms.py` suppresses `share_removed` when the pooled reference is below 0.01 of the achievable improvement or negative on any landscape, in cells, overall, per benchmark and per acquisition; a landscape whose reference is exactly zero does not suppress.

Report the price of every process change beside its recovery, and compare prices only across the same acquisitions. The cautious rule's 0.015 is over ten acquisitions; on LogEI and qNEI, where the replayed remedies (rank refit, shortlist, LUCB) run, it is 0.022 and the rank refit's is 0.027. LCB1 and LCB2 are not free for LogEI and qNEI (0.021–0.023 of `opt_z`, more than the two-design sitting's 0.015–0.018). A sitting's price depends on k only, because the clean twin is shared by every cell and process, and it is not monotone in k: 0.015, 0.010, 0.012, 0.015, 0.022, 0.028 for k = 2, 3, 5, 8, 12, 16 on seeds 7–16. A price is `budget_split.py --twin clean` or the arm's `trt_clean − ref_clean`; never use the gain at 0.05σ as a price.

Never report an acquisition robustness ranking without naming the incumbent. Against its own clean twin, the observed-max incumbent removes 20.0% of the summed post-onset trajectory loss in `opt_z` units (15.6% in landscape SDs) and 40% for LogPI, and moves UCB and qNEI by exactly 0.000 because they never read `best_f`. That zero is the control that validates the pairing. Against the standard process it recovers 19% [10, 27] of the trajectory loss and makes the deployed design worse by 10% [5, 15] of its cost. Every ranking here ranks acquisition-and-incumbent pairs.

Do not re-raise the GP-noise confound. Supplying the true injected variance as `train_Yvar` removes 3.4% of the summed post-onset trajectory loss against its own clean twin in `opt_z` units (5.0% in landscape SDs); against the standard process it changes the cost by −3% on both the trajectory and the deployed design; nothing survives FDR correction, and the onset ratio does not shrink. The onset effect remains unexplained, and the text should say so.

`random` and `sobol` must show 0.000e+00 excess search regret under response error. They ignore observations, so any other number means the pairing is broken. Their deployed design is still chosen by noisy ratings, so they are no zero control on the deployed metric, and under input error they evaluate displaced points and are no zero control at all. A claim about the floors on the deployed design concerns their ABSOLUTE deployed regret (`checks/floor_deployed.py`, 100,000 landscape resamples plus a t test and a signed-rank test, each with Holm over the ten acquisitions): at 5σ from the first rating only LogPI beats the floor, and only by the bootstrap (adjusted p 0.03; t 0.10, signed-rank 0.12); at 1σ six acquisitions beat it under all three tests. `tab:acq`'s deployed column is the excess, on which the floors always look best: their search excess is exactly zero, so their deployed excess is selection loss alone, and that selection loss is at or near the top of the acquisitions' range (1σ from the first rating: 0.178 against 0.105 to 0.164; 5σ: 0.501 against 0.312 to 0.520). Their clean run already ships a poor design (0.348 of `opt_z`).

Aggregate as ratios of landscape means with a landscape bootstrap, never as means of per-run ratios. The suite's achievable improvement (`opt_z`) spans 74×, from 0.76 on `powell` to 56.8 on `shekel`, so per-run ratios let a landscape with tiny regret outvote one with large regret. The landscapes share their random numbers by design, so state that the bootstrap treats correlated clusters as independent. With three or four clusters (the fitted-oracle datasets, the halo problems), report the cluster count and the per-dataset values (`output-boba/analysis/adaptations_per_dataset.csv`), and do not star results: with three clusters the percentile bootstrap is exactly the min–max range, and the exact two-sided Wilcoxon cannot go below 2/2^n, 0.25 at three clusters and 0.125 at four.

A pooled onset ratio is a ratio of landscape means, so its denominator weights the landscapes: the scalar Gaussian 1σ ratio is 9.6× on the floor gap and 5.5× on `opt_z`. Compare arms on one basis and divide by the running-maximum bound (`onset_bound.py`; `checks/mo_onset_bound.py` for the multi-objective arm).

Name the unit of every regression and variance share. Every mediation R² in `mediator_model.csv` and `tab:mediation` is on `excess_sd`, the post-onset excess in landscape SDs; `mediator_model_normalised.csv` holds the `opt_z`-free rows on both units. frag alone beats the magnitude alone only in landscape SDs (0.52 against 0.16; on the fraction of `opt_z` 0.46 against 0.51), and on the fraction of `opt_z` frag adds to the magnitude only when it is left in landscape SDs (+0.022 [0.007, 0.066]; divided by `opt_z`, +0.002 [−0.018, +0.021]). Never refit the descriptor rows on fragility. `factor_variance_shares.csv` has a `response` column, and the order of the factors reverses between units: on the fraction of `opt_z` the magnitude leads (24%) and the landscape takes 10%; in landscape SDs the landscape leads (32%) and the magnitude takes 10%. `descriptor_vif.csv` holds the VIFs of the PRIMARY descriptor model's own design, indicators included (log `opt_z` 1.2), and is what `tab:descriptors` prints; the seven-descriptor VIFs (log `opt_z` 9.2, sparsity 5.8, skew 6.3) are in `descriptor_vif_all.csv`, and the 9.2 is quoted only as "with sparsity and skew added".

Name the Holm family with every adjusted p. `heldout_remedies.py` tests each procedure in its own scope (column `scope` of section `4_holm`). The fixed k, the derived budget rule and the best-arm (LUCB) sittings are only in the 41-member "all" family, not the 27-member core family, and the rank rules are tested on the main sweep, not on the spike and ceiling arms that give their headline recoveries. Every LUCB number before 2026-10-09 is void: the bound was NaN, so each run showed k/2 + 1 designs in rank order. The corrected rule (`replay_hitl_remedies.py`) bounds an unseen candidate by the prefix GP anchored to the looks already made (universal kriging, the sitting's shared offset unknown) and a seen one by its mean look ± s/√n, with a width of one SD fixed in advance; each look goes to the leader or its strongest challenger, the pool is the top 3k (`pool_factor` 3), and its first look at each of the top k shares the tournament's draw. No LUCB sitting survives Holm, and `heldout_remedies.py` section 4d shows each behind one look each at the same k and noise (ahead on at most 5 of 20 landscapes); the sitting of eight trails by +0.014 of `opt_z` pooled, 80% of it at 5σ from trial 21 (`checks/lucb_shortfall_by_cell.py`). Quote it as evidence that the looks pay by reaching more designs, never as a remedy. The analyse_ship_rules BH family is arm × rule × error process across that process's variants (`fdr_family_size`); until 2026-10-09 the bias variants (`bias0.05` … `bias5`) were corrected over two cells each. The known-variance rule, the gated sittings, F1, the decision map, the winner's curse and the untouched k = 16 test are post hoc and in no Holm family of the paper; quote each with its own family (the eight cells of one procedure or level).

Fitted-oracle arms: every run seed refits the oracle (`bo_sensor_error_simulation.py`, `build_oracle(seed=seed)`), so `y_opt`, the model-free floor, the oracle's mean and `sigma_f` all differ between the seeds of one dataset or landscape. Form every normaliser within each seed and aggregate as mean_s(excess_s)/mean_s(normaliser_s); never take the maximum clean best over seeds (`oracle_isolation.seed_optima`/`floor_gap`, `analyse_fitted_companion.fitted_optimum`/`floor_gap`/`fitted_achievable`, `checks/oracle_achievable.seed_achievable`/`sigma_f_achievable`/`exact_best_clean`). Compare a fitted arm with the exact arm on the share of the achievable improvement, not on the floor gap: for the exact arm that is `opt_z`, for the fitted arm the seed's `y_opt` less that oracle's mean over the search box. The floor gap is 1.1–1.7% of `opt_z` on Branin, Powell and Rosenbrock, which is what carried the withdrawn "about a quarter" (a floor-gap ratio of landscape means, 0.20 [0.11, 0.62]), and a tree oracle's is inflated by an optimum a median 17% of its achievable improvement above the best clean value. Each family sets its own achievable improvement (a tree's `y_opt` − mean is a median 1.27 × `sigma_f` × `opt_z`, a GP's or MLP's about 1), so a claim about oracle families must hold under all four normalisers (primary, `best_clean`, `sigma_f`, `both_best_clean`); the closing table of `checks/oracle_families.py` shows them side by side. The tree's search-loss understatement (0.72 [0.56, 0.93]) is significant under only two of the four; the MLP reports more search loss than the trees under all four; on the deployed design the family contrasts change sign between normalisers at 1σ and 5σ. Write "similar median fidelity", never "equal fidelity".

`gp.likelihood.noise` is the noise variance of the standardised targets. Multiply it by `gp.outcome_transform.stdvs ** 2` before combining it with anything in objective units.

Centre variables before forming interaction or quadratic terms, otherwise the linear coefficient is a conditional slope at zero of the raw variable.

Record the command behind every table in `paper/COMMANDS.md`. A number without a recorded producer does not go into the paper.

## Traps that have cost real time

A run's variant lives only in its file name. One output directory can hold several spike sizes or two ceiling modes. `run_metadata.json` and the per-arm summary CSVs are overwritten by the last invocation, so they describe only the last variant. `evaluate_research_question.py` parses the suffix into a `variant` column and puts it in `CONDITION_COLS`.

The file-name suffix now also carries the rating instrument: `_clip<lo>,<hi>`, `_clip-sample` and `_round<q>`, as the last parts of the suffix and only when those flags are set, so standard runs keep their names. `output-boba-instrument` and `output-boba-spike-clip` hold clipped or rounded runs under STANDARD file names, because they ran before the name carried the instrument. Never resume them in place: `_guard_instrument_naming` refuses such a directory, so use a fresh `--output-dir`, and then update every analysis that pairs by name (`analyse_boba_adaptations.py`'s `spike-clip-*` arms need `variant=f"{_sp}_clip-sample"` with `ref_variant` staying `_sp`; the instrument arm's evaluation will carry the variant `clip-8,8_round0.55`; `make_boba_paper_tables.py` reads `output-boba-instrument/analysis`).

A spike run's sitting noise comes from its file-name variant `sp<p>-<size>`, where the size is the spike SD actually used, never from `run_metadata.json`. `build_tasks` puts a stem's single spike variant into the task's `Settings.spike_variant`, and `sitting_sd_fn` falls back on it when a caller passes no `variant=` (`replay_hitl_remedies.py` passes none); a stem that mixes spike variants raises. The SD-only path gets sqrt(σ² + p s²) = 7.75 for `sp0.15-20` (it was 0.268 before the fix, so the look-buying rows of `output-boba-spike/analysis/hitl_remedies{,_heldout}` on disk are stale; the corrected ones are in `hitl_remedies_spikesd{,_heldout}`). LUCB looks take only an SD, so under spikes they are Gaussian at the marginal SD. The capped-scale arm replays its looks without the cap, so its look-buying rows are optimistic in the same way; only its rows that buy no look are quoted.

A variant that changes the clean run needs its own `--output-dir`. The simulator's guard (`_clean_run_settings` in `bo_synthetic_error_simulation.py`) tracks only minimum distance, the input-uncertain acquisition, relevance pursuit, the rater model and the halo model. It does not track the Student-t likelihood, the noisy-input GP, `--replicate-first`, `--final-rerate` or `--inference-rule`, so check those by hand.

Rerunning an arm into its old directory reuses its old runs. The drivers skip a directory that contains `SWEEP_COMPLETE`, and every run uses `--resume`. Since 2026-10-23 `--resume` also checks each log's `run_config_fingerprint` (the whole `SimulationConfig`, the acquisition, the driver's and the core's sha256 and the numerical environment) and refuses a mismatch, so code changes need a fresh directory. A clean twin's fingerprint holds the error settings too, although the clean run never uses them. A directory whose variants of one error process share clean twins (`output-boba-spike`, `-ceiling`, `-relay`, all run before the fingerprint) therefore cannot take another variant: the second spike size stops at the first clean twin it meets. Give every new variant its own directory, as `run_boba_cliprun.sh` does (`output-boba-cliprun2-<variant>`), and pair across directories in `analyse_boba_adaptations.ARMS`. Run logs written before the fingerprint have none, so `--resume` refuses them too (`run_oracle_isolation.ps1` now refuses its own arms); `--allow-legacy-resume` admits them only after their settings and code have been checked by hand.

`drift_down` (appended 2026-11-07) is the drift ramp with its sign reversed. It takes drift's entry in the noise seed (`sim.error_model_seed_index`), so a downward and an upward drift run of one landscape, acquisition, cell and seed draw the same jitter and differ only in the ramp; any script that re-derives a run's noise stream must call that function, never `ERROR_MODEL_CHOICES.index`. `--error-models all` still means the six models before it (`sim.ALL_ERROR_MODELS`), so no driver that asks for "all" changed. `replay_end_of_study.py` handles it under both sitting processes. Its name contains an underscore, so a file-name parser that splits the model at `_` reads it as "down": `analyse_extra_runs.JITTERED`, which the replays and `replay_stopping.py` use, names it explicitly beside `missing_mcar` and `missing_low`, and any new parser must do the same.

`--gp-prior legacy` (2026-11-07) replaces BoTorch's default surrogate (RBF with dimension-scaled log-normal priors) by BoTorch's default before 0.12 (a scaled Matérn-5/2 ARD kernel, Gamma(3, 6) lengthscale and Gamma(2, 0.15) outputscale priors, Gamma(1.1, 0.05) noise prior), built by BoTorch's own helpers through `sim.gp_prior_modules`. It changes the clean run (`_clean_run_settings` records it), so it needs its own directory. `rescore_ship_rules.py` refits with the arm's prior (read from the `args` of `run_metadata.json`, not a top-level key; its `gp_kernel` column says `ScaleKernel` for the legacy arm and `RBFKernel` for the default, which is the quick check); `replay_end_of_study.py` refuses any prior but the default, so the legacy arm has no sitting replays. The default path builds exactly what it built before (`tests/test_regression_logged_run.py`).

`--response-clip running<c>` clips each rating after the initial design to c robust SDs (1.4826 MAD, the SD when the MAD is zero, at least three ratings) beyond the range of the ratings recorded so far. Unlike `--response-clip sample` it acts on the clean twin as well (`_clean_run_settings` records it), so it is a process change with a measured price and needs its own directory. Its noisy files carry `_clip-run<c>`; its clean twins carry no suffix.

A baseline's oracle name can contain an underscore (`extra_trees`). Parse it from the logged `oracle_model`, never by regex over the file name.

A run's `y_opt` is not a column of `paired_excess_metrics.csv`. It is `final_best_true_baseline + final_simple_regret_true_baseline`, constant within a seed; for the exact arm it equals `opt_z`, which `oracle_achievable.exact_best_clean` checks. An oracle's mean over the box can be estimated without refitting, from the designs of each seed's clean random and Sobol floor runs (`analyse_fitted_companion.box_means`); on the isolation arms that moves no ratio by more than 0.013.

The oracle-isolation error grids and `corr_oracle_truth` come from the seed-7 oracle only. The GP and MLP arms' other seeds differ in `sigma_f` by 0.57–1.98× (`checks/oracle_sigmaf_seeds.py`). The per-seed arms (`output-oracle-iso*-perseed`, below) rerun every seed on its own grid; quote them beside the published arms, never in their place, because the paper's tables are the seed-7 ones. Run the oracle checks in the order `oracle_sigmaf_seeds`, `oracle_achievable`, `oracle_families`: each reads the one before. With four acquisitions and twenty landscapes, per-landscape best-acquisition agreement is at chance even for the exact arm against itself across seed halves (16/60 on the trajectory, 17/60 on the deployed design), so do not cite it as evidence. Each oracle-isolation arm holds 2,400 run files (600 clean, 1,800 noisy) under `runs/`; the other CSVs there are summaries.

The per-seed oracle-isolation arms (`output-oracle-iso{,-gaussian_process,-mlp}-perseed`, `run_oracle_isolation_perseed.py`) scale each seed's grid to that seed's own oracle. Their clean twins are the published ones, copied after `check-clean` reran one per family exactly; the copies carry no fingerprint, so the runner resumes their directories with `--allow-legacy-resume` and only after checking every unfingerprinted file against `clean_copies.csv`. `run` refuses to continue once `bo_sensor_error_simulation.py` has changed since `check-clean` or a finished invocation, so never edit the core while it runs. `oracle_isolation._check_balanced` compares run counts only among the seeds present, so a cell holding one seed passes; the per-seed arms are guarded by the runner's grid check and a `calibration` marker instead. `oracle_isolation_acq_ranking.py` reads one grid per landscape and the per-invocation summary CSVs, so it is not valid for the per-seed arms and fails on them. `calibrate()` imports the simulator after numpy, so the simulator's one-thread settings come too late; that is the source of differences of 1e-15 on seed 7. `checks/oracle_perseed.py` first reruns the published pipeline into a scratch directory and stops unless every file is byte-identical.

`replay_end_of_study.load_per_run` re-reads the per-stem files with pandas' default float parser, which is not round-trip exact (up to 7e-15 against `end_of_study_per_run.csv.gz`). A bit-for-bit comparison with a published per-run table must take the same round trip or read with `float_precision="round_trip"`; `checks/sitting_order_effects.py` does both.

CatBoost writes `catboost_info/` into the working directory unless told not to. Oracles are now built with `allow_writing_files=False` (fits are bit-identical), and in-process fits of the oracle-isolation pipeline go through `oracle_isolation.fit_outside_repo`. The tracked `catboost_info/` folder is a leftover.

`analyse_boba_adaptations.paired_frame` raises for a dataset without a scale (`KeyError`) or with a non-positive one (`ValueError`); it no longer falls back to 1.0. Every `ARMS` entry names its scale with `arm(..., scale=...)`: `opt_z`, `hv_floor_gap` (the multi-objective floor gap, `analyse_boba_mo.achievable_gain` on `output-boba-mo`) or `fitted_achievable` (`analyse_fitted_companion.fitted_achievable` on `output-fitted`), and `dataset_scale(kind)` computes each. A caller that passed `{}` for a relative arm, such as `checks/tie_break.py`, must pass `aba.dataset_scale(spec['scale'])`. The pooled BH family in `adaptations_recovery.csv` spans every arm, so rescaling or adding one arm moves the other arms' pooled q values, but never their recovery or intervals.

The tie-break check has two steps. `tie_break.py scan` reads every per-run log (about 15 minutes on 6 workers) and writes `review/tie_break/tie_runs.parquet`, which is git-ignored; `tie_break.py rescore` reads only that parquet. After any new run logs, rerun the scan first.

`replay_end_of_study.py --sitting-process shared` (the default) is the model of every sitting number in the paper: a simultaneous sitting whose bias, drift ramp and AR(1) state cancel. `--sitting-process sequential` continues the ramp and the AR(1) state (from the logged `error_magnitude` at trial T − k) over trials T − k + 1 … T and changes only the drift and AR(1) rows; `--sitting-order rank` shows the candidates best-ranked first. Each needs its own `--output-dir`. `settings.json` records `sitting_process` and `sitting_order` only when they are not the default, so old directories still match their settings. `replay_hitl_remedies.py` has no `--sitting-process`, so the LUCB sittings exist under the shared model only. `checks/sitting_sequential.py` is the process sensitivity; its shared side must reproduce the published outputs before it prints a sequential value, and it exits non-zero otherwise, so rerun it after any change to `sitting_by_magnitude.py`, `heldout_remedies.py` or their inputs.

`sitting_by_magnitude.py --replay-dirs` takes `dir=drift+ar1` to read only those error processes from a directory, and filtered entries must partition the runs; every tagged run records its replay directories in a `provenance` entry of its selection JSON. Where one quantity is printed by two generators, the means agree and the intervals differ in the third decimal (the first and reference blocks of `fresh_seed_replication.txt`; the selection JSON and the per-k CSV rows of `sitting_by_magnitude`). Quote one source consistently: the paper quotes the selection JSON (which the table prints), the per-k CSV for a k that was not chosen, and the first block of the fresh check for the sitting's gain.

## What is settled, and what is not

An arm that replaces the acquisition is scored against LogEI, never against the mean of the ten standard acquisitions: that reference carries the four weakest arms' cost and produced a recovery above 100% in one cell. The `*-ten` companions keep the other version visible.

Settled, with the measurement. Selection is 41.8% [34, 50] of the deployed cost of error at 1σ from the first rating and 83.1% [75, 90] at 5σ from trial 21. No acquisition-side change tested improved the deployed design, and augmented EI does not improve the trajectory either once it is scored against LogEI (+1% [-18, +18]). Early replication and Thompson sampling make the deployed design worse.

The end of the study. At 1σ from the first rating the zero-trial LCB1 rule does the work: 10% [2, 18] of the deployed cost held out (four processes pooled), 14% [5, 21] untouched (Gaussian), 4% [−2, 11] on seeds 7–11, price 0.021–0.023. A final sitting of two adds +0.003 [−0.003, +0.009] held out and −0.008 [−0.017, +0.002] untouched (Gaussian); its gain over the standard process (+0.030 [0.012, 0.050], 12% [5, 18], four processes held out) survives Holm over the nine sizes, and its increment over either zero-trial rule survives no correction. At 5σ from trial 21 a sitting of sixteen gains +0.227 [0.147, 0.321] held out (41% of the deployed cost, four processes pooled) and +0.249 [0.164, 0.339] over LCB1, and it holds when the drift ramp and the AR(1) state move through the sitting (+0.226 with the candidates in random order, +0.212 in rank order); the advantage comes mostly from drift, AR(1) and bias, and under Gaussian error alone the increment over LCB1 is +0.097 [0.030, 0.162] (Holm over k 0.12). On the untouched seeds a sitting of twelve beats LCB1 by +0.111 [0.048, 0.174] in that cell (Gaussian), and the sitting of sixteen, replayed there post hoc, by +0.121 [0.064, 0.182] (32% [23, 40] of the cost, price 0.030). Gated on the GP's noise SD, the sitting of sixteen keeps +0.190 of that gain held out at a price of 0.000, and F1 recovers 53% at 0.009 against the freeze rule's 52% (post hoc). The replication under all four processes on seeds 27–36 (E1 of the review round, analysis frozen in `configs/review_round_plan.json` before its runs; `checks/review_experiments.py`) confirms all four planned tests (Holm over them). LCB2 gains +0.018 [0.002, 0.032] at 1σ from the first rating (7.4% [1, 13], p 0.019), though the landscape × seed bootstrap widens that to [−0.002, +0.036]. The sitting of sixteen gains +0.231 [0.165, 0.300] at 5σ from trial 21 (41.5% [33, 49]), +0.223 over LCB1 and +0.199 over LCB2, and gains under every process. At 1σ, LCB1 recovers 8.1% [2, 15] and the sitting of two 8.0%, 0.000 over LCB1. Its 800 clean twins equal the confirmatory arm's run for run.

The drift's direction matters (E2, `drift_down`, seeds 7–16, LogEI and qNEI, paired with the upward runs).

## Before you claim a result

1. Run `python -m pytest tests/ -q` and keep the log.
2. Run `python scripts/check_paper.py --paper paper`. It checks inputs, references and table shapes, and it does not compile the document.
3. Compile the paper (`pdflatex`, `bibtex`, then `pdflatex` three times from a clean directory). It is a TMLR submission in the official TMLR style: no page limit, but an unusually long main text slows the review, so check where the main text through Limitations ends. `python paper/build_overleaf_bundle.py` does this in a clean directory, collects the figures, repeats `pdflatex` until the log asks for no rerun, and prints the length; the zip it writes is the Overleaf upload.
4. Regenerate tables with `python scripts/make_boba_paper_tables.py --analysis output-boba/analysis` after any analysis change, and `python scripts/sitting_by_magnitude.py` after any change to the sitting's inputs (it writes `paper/tables/sitting_by_magnitude.tex`). Never edit generated files in `paper/tables/`.
5. Regenerate figures with `python scripts/make_paper_figures.py` after any analysis change, and look at the PNGs it writes beside the PDFs before committing.
6. Record every new command in `paper/COMMANDS.md`, in run order, with the outputs it writes.
