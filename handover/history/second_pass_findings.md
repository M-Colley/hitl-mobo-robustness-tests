## D. New findings that change a number, a claim, or the ability to reproduce

### D1. VERIFIED. The two "asserted by test" claims in the Reproducibility statement skip on every machine except the author's

`tests/test_boba_benchmarks.py:36-37` hard-codes `BOBA_ROOT = Path("C:/Users/markc/Desktop/BOBA")` and gates the 1e-9 parity test against BOBA's implementations and the provenance test for the stale `power_law_practice` optimum behind `HAS_BOBA`. A reviewer running `pytest tests/` sees 21 SKIPPED. main.tex:667-675 says both are "asserted by test". Fix. Vendor BOBA's reference outputs on the 40 random points into `tests/fixtures/` and make the tests unconditional, or reword the paper to "checked against the BOBA checkout at commit X on 2026-09-06" and record that commit.

### D2. VERIFIED. The test suite cannot be collected clean as shipped

`tests/test_hierarchical_oracle.py:38` loads `scripts/hierarchical_oracle_test.py`, which imports `jax` and `numpyro` unconditionally. Neither is in any requirements file. The module fails at collection, and pytest then aborts the whole run with "Interrupted: 1 error during collection", so `python -m pytest tests/` runs no test at all on a machine without those packages (corrected and confirmed by execution in the third pass). CI installs neither package, so its test step should abort too. The hierarchical oracle appears nowhere in the paper. Add a module-level `pytest.importorskip("numpyro")`, which turns the failure into one skipped module. After that fix, a reviewer's machine will additionally show the 21 BOBA parity skips (D1) and 4 skips on paths under the author's temporary directory.

### D3. VERIFIED. The datasets and tests embed the authors' GitHub identity

`datasets.json:4,29,56`, `datasets-ehmi.json`, `datasets-provoice.json` and `datasets-extended.json` point to GitHub repositories under the authors' account names and a third party's account. `tests/test_cli_smoke.py:41,55` and `tests/test_simulation_utils.py:597-599` embed the same URLs as fixtures. The name of one data repository contains the venue and year of the authors' prior paper. The simulator records the commit of each cloned data directory in `data_dir_commits` (bo_sensor_error_simulation.py:4828-4843), but a reused cache silently supplies whatever commit it holds, and `calibrate_noise_from_data.py` and `anchor_noise_scale.py` record no provenance. README advises quoting the commits in the paper, which must wait for the camera-ready version (corrected in the third pass). No licence is documented for any of the three datasets. Before the supplementary, replace the URLs with an anonymised snapshot, ship the CSVs with licence statements where allowed, and replace the URLs in tests with placeholders.

### D4. VERIFIED. The provoice rows use a composite the project's own docs call a bug

`docs/oracle-fidelity-experiments-2026-06-12.md:21`. Mental Demand spans 1 to 17 against 1 to 5 for the other two constructs, so the unweighted composite is 80.7% negative mental demand (r = 0.965). The documented fix is `--normalize-objective`. The fitted arm as run for the paper never passes that flag (`run_boba_gaps_rest.ps1:151-157`, `run_boba_gaps2.ps1:114-121`), `anchor_noise_scale.py:80` hard-codes `normalize=False`, and `calibrate_noise_from_data.py` defaults to raw ratings. The provoice row of Table 20 (1.02), the provoice third of Appendix C and the fitted row of Table 26 therefore describe the mental-demand surface. `run_provoice_normalized.ps1` is a legacy driver with a different design (20 seeds, the default error models, onsets 10, 20 and 40, a grid of 0.033 to 0.26) and cannot reproduce the companion arm (corrected in the third pass). A normalised rerun needs a new driver that mirrors the fitted-arm sections of the gap drivers, plus a code change in `anchor_noise_scale.py`. The alternative is to drop provoice from Table 20 and Appendix C and say why. The provoice oracle's held-out R² is −0.03 (third pass I8).

### D5. RELAYED, consistent with code read. Table 20's denominator is not one quantity across the three studies

ehmi uses `oracle_target: "mean"` (datasets.json:5), so its σf is the SD of the population-mean surface, which is smoothed and small and biases the ratio 0.74 upward, as the paper says. provoice uses the `individual` target with an ExtraTrees oracle at `min_samples_leaf=2`, and every row is tripled by jitter augmentation, so a leaf can hold one rating and its two clones. That oracle interpolates individual noisy ratings, its σf contains rater-noise variance, and its ratio is biased downward, the opposite direction. opticarvis also uses the `individual` target, but its deployed oracle is gradient boosting with depth 3, so the memorisation argument does not apply to it (corrected in the third pass). Its opt_z of 13.36 can come from the anchoring of y_opt on training rows (`estimate_oracle_optimum`, lines 1464-1489) or from a search box that extends beyond the rated designs (`bounds_from_data`, third pass I13). The paper states only the first bias direction (main.tex:327-329). Use one kind of surface for all three studies, report the estimator per row and state both bias directions, or move the "12–20%" anchor out of the abstract.

### D6. VERIFIED. The multi-objective "absolute loss" column is the excess fraction

`analyse_boba_mo.py:140` sets `frac = excess / gain` where the numerator is `auc_simple_regret_excess_true_postonset_per_iter`. `acquisition_agreement` averages it as `mean_frac` and `make_boba_paper_tables.py:439-446` prints it under the header "absolute loss". The scalar Table 4's "absolute loss" is the noisy run's own regret over opt_z (analyse_boba_robustness.py:182). The mean of the eight MO dose cells in tables/multiobjective.tex is 20.5, confirming the column is the excess. Relabel the MO column "excess" or compute the absolute quantity. No test imports analyse_boba_mo.

### D7. VERIFIED by grep. Three MO front sentences and the freeze-rule ceiling come from no script

main.tex:1106-1110 "about half of it is truly dominated (5.2 of 10.9 designs), it misses most of the truly non-dominated designs evaluated, and its hypervolume shortfall exceeds that of the evaluated set in every cell". `replay_mo_front.py:9-10` attributes the first to "an independent recomputation" not in the repository, computes no recall and no shortfall comparison. Appendix P's "freeze ceiling falls from 82% to 54% after two trials of delay" is produced by neither `replay_stopping.py` nor `changepoint_compare.py`, which have no delay sweep. `changepoint_compare.py:5-6` quotes "recovers 42 to 64 per cent" for the freeze rule, which is in no table. Remove the sentences or add the scripts.

### D8. VERIFIED by reasoning on `analyse_extra_runs.py:139-144`. Late-onset "no extra trials" is zero by construction for most runs

`origin` is the first trial at which the clean run reached its k-trial target. A late-onset noisy run is identical to its clean twin through the onset, so whenever origin ≤ onset the noisy run reaches the target at origin and extra = 0 regardless of the error. For Table 2's right column (onset 40, k = 50, 1% tolerance) every run whose clean twin plateaued within 1% of its trial-50 regret by trial 40 scores 0 by construction. main.tex:341 presents "Error from trial 41 costs a 50-trial study no extra trials at the median" as a finding. The "8–12% never recover" is the informative part. Report the share of runs with origin ≤ onset, or restrict the late-onset cell to runs whose clean twin improved after the onset.

### D9. RELAYED. "Kendall's W 0.056 against 0.52 for the scalar suite computed the same way" (main.tex:1124-1126) has no scalar counterpart in any script

The scalar analysis computes W per condition (median 0.247); the MO analysis computes W on condition-averaged fractions. No condition-averaged scalar W is produced anywhere. Compute it or drop the comparison.

### D10. VERIFIED by driver read. The halo-backfit row of Table 25 does not match the appendix's description of the arms

main.tex:1462-1464 says every budget-neutral arm runs twenty landscapes, seeds 7 to 11, both onsets. The halo arms run four MO problems, one acquisition (qLogNEHVI), one magnitude, seeds 7 to 16 (`run_boba_budget_neutral.ps1:173`, `analyse_boba_adaptations.py:180-183`). The interval "+25% [7, 50]" is a bootstrap over four clusters, which has 35 distinct resamples (corrected in the third pass) and the Wilcoxon over four problems has a minimum p of 0.125. State the arm's design in the caption or move the row to Appendix E.

## E. Adversarial-read findings on the prose (an area chair's list)

E1. The paper attacks an uncited "standard construction" (main.tex:45-46, 103, 106). No citation names a paper that fits regression oracles to archival ratings; the motivating studies are a TODO. A reviewer reads this as a strawman or as the authors' own prior work. Cite two third-party instances or reframe as "a common construction, including in our own prior work" after de-anonymisation.

E2. The abstract's best positive number (fault models "worth 22–38% at near-zero price") rests on remedies and fault models never defined in the paper. None of spiking, per-rater handover, halo, saturating cap, relevance pursuit, Thompson sampling, LCB incumbent, minimum-distance or slip-aware acquisition is defined in Sections 3, 11 or any appendix, Table 25 has no price column, and the "−979%" imputation row has no interval. Add an appendix defining each remedy with its equation and citation, add the price column, drop the imputation row. Hours to a day.

E3. "The one term an acquisition function cannot reach" (abstract, contribution 3, main.tex:369-371) is stated as a structural fact. The acquisition chooses the visited set and where the noisy ratings are taken, so max over V of f minus f(d) depends on it. The honest claim is that no acquisition-side change tested reduced the selection loss. "The reason is structural" at main.tex:395-396 has the same problem. The earlier version of this item argued numerically that augmented EI's 41% trajectory recovery and 2% deployed recovery imply a rise in selection loss. That argument was invalid, because the trajectory metric is a post-onset time average of search loss and the deployed metric is a final value (corrected in the third pass). Augmented EI is also scored against a ten-acquisition mean (third pass I6).

E4. Every headline pooled share is excess-weighted and therefore dominated by the 5σ cells the paper places outside the realistic range. 61.8% rises from 29% at 0.05σ to 82% at 5σ late; the share at 1σ from trial 1, the anchored realistic cell, is never given. Same construction for 5.0% (known noise; Table 9 shows +39%, +43%, +17% in individual cells), 15.6% (incumbent), and "at most half as much" (MO; intervals overlap at 1σ and 5σ). The abstract's "so the loss is informational" is drawn from a pooled null with no interval. Report each share at 1σ, onset 0, beside the pool.

E5. Section 6's power law is fitted on four magnitude levels and Appendix B then finds the response quadratic in log σe. If log E[R] is curved in log σe, βc is not defined and the GAIN test βc + βz = 1 tests a form the paper rejects two appendices later. Quasi-Poisson on cell means that can be negative is defensible as Poisson pseudo-maximum-likelihood but must be named and cited (Gourieroux, Monfort and Trognon 1984; Santos Silva and Tenreyro 2006). Downgrade Section 6 to "SPREAD fails" (βz ≠ 0 is robust) and drop the GAIN verdict.

E6. "Dimension does raise the cost when opt_z is held fixed" is a two-point comparison (d = 7 to 11) with no interval, after excluding the d = 4 rung that is higher than d = 7. At 0.25σ the ladder is also non-monotone. "0.246 to 0.245, no effect at all" asserts an exact null from a 5-seed arm with no interval. Add bootstrap intervals to Table 12 and soften both sentences.

E7. The factor-importance ranking (abstract, main.tex:306-311) compares range ratios across factors with 20, 2, 10 and 4 levels. Range ratios grow with the number of levels. The paper's own variance shares are 0.24 for magnitude, 0.17 for onset, 0.10 for landscape, 0.04 for acquisition and 0.002 for the error process (main.tex:311), so onset outranks landscape (labels corrected in the third pass). The abstract ranks landscape first. Landscape is observational, so "moves" is causal language. Lead with the variance shares.

E8. Section 9 reports the best of 19 replayed variants with an uncorrected interval (winner's curse), the k-curve values at k = 12, 16, 20 (+0.046, +0.051, +0.042) cannot locate a peak, "about a third of the budget" is not resolved by the data (anything from k = 8 to 20 fits), the replays' acquisitions and seeds are never stated, and the 98.3% / 15.7% / 0.5% false-claim rates have no denominator and no interval.

E9. The frag claim compares in-sample R² 0.52 to an additive comparator (0.53) the paper itself declares misspecified (interaction model 0.78). No σe-only baseline is reported, yet Appendix R admits within-magnitude Spearman is 0.08 to 0.45, so most of the pooled 0.85 is the magnitude. The "ten-trial pilot" is computed on a CLEAN LogEI run; a real pilot has noisy ratings. Add a leave-one-landscape-out R² column, a log σe-only row, and a noisy-pilot row.

E10. Uncited methods the paper runs. Relevance pursuit (Ament et al. 2024), Student-t GP (Martinez-Cantin et al. 2018; Jylänki et al. 2011), noisy-input GP (McHutchon and Rasmussen 2011; Fröhlich et al. 2020; Oliveira et al. 2019), continuous qKG (Wu and Frazier 2016; the cited frazier2009 is the discrete KG), Thompson sampling, CUSUM (Page 1954), corruption-tolerant BO (Bogunovic et al. 2020, exactly the spiking and misclick setting). `audibert2010best` is in the bib and uncited. The HITL BO literature the introduction gestures at (Khajah 2016, Koyama 2017/2020, Dudley 2019, Kadner 2021, Chan 2022, Liao 2023) is absent. `eggensperger2021hpobench` is cited for "results on analytic test functions need not transfer", which should be checked against the HPOBench paper (unverified here). The noisy-input GP arm implements McHutchon and Rasmussen (2011). Fröhlich et al. (2020) is related robust-BO work (attribution corrected in the third pass). All 25 existing bib entries check out on venue, year and pages.

E11. Abstract numbers that live only in an appendix are 15.7% → 0.5% (Appendix M), 22–38% (Appendix N, given there as two ranges) and 1.6× (Appendix A). "Near-zero price" has no table behind it, because Table 25 has no price column (third pass I7 computes several prices). "Seven acquisition-side changes" matches the seven kinds of change behind Table 25's twelve rows once the kinds are named. The earlier version of this item also called Table 23's "evaluated" column an oracle the person cannot identify. Ratings are exact in the input-error arms, so the best-rated trial's actual design is the best evaluated design and the person can identify it (retracted in the third pass).

E12. AI use statement. ICLR 2027 requires a section following the template "In this work, we used generative AI tools for <required-disclosure tasks>. We have not used generative AI tools for <other tasks>..." plus how outputs were verified. The current "LLM usage" section cites the 2026 policy. It describes design, implementation and analysis uses in free prose, but it states no negative list and no verification method (corrected in the third pass). The template may be adapted, and the verification may list only checks actually performed. Authors must write it; the same text goes in the submission form.

E13. No ethics or data statement for three archival human-rating datasets (source, licence, consent, IRB at collection, de-identification, public or not). ICLR's ethics statement is optional; a reviewer flag on undocumented human-subject data is routine. One paragraph, authors only.

E14. Intervals including zero or unstated, worded as effects. "None improves the deployed design: augmented EI +2% [−6, +11]" (upper bound 11%). "The share is the same for every error process (60.7–63.6%)" without intervals. "The four hypervolume acquisitions are indistinguishable" in the main text as fact, "no detectable difference" in the appendix. Table 3 DRIFT "gain" verdicts on intervals touching zero. Budget ratios 2.3×/5.1×/11.7× without intervals from 5 seeds, and at 0.05σ the ratios are 2.2×/4.4×/1.4×, so "grows with the budget" holds only at ≥ 0.25σ.

E15. Twenty clusters everywhere with up to thirteen-parameter models (Table 6 "both + int."); ordinary cluster and BCa bootstrap coverage is poor at that ratio. A wild cluster bootstrap or the cluster count stated beside every interval is expected. BH applied within families of 5 to 9 tests while several hundred starred intervals appear across ~30 arms; state the family structure and the absence of cross-arm correction.

E16. Claims true by construction presented as findings: the comparison loop's drift invariance (re-perceived at the current sitting, drift consumes no RNG) and BIAS at onset 0 moving neither loop. Say "by construction" and move to Appendix O.

E17. Internal contradictions. "The suite was assembled for a separate study and adopted unchanged" versus one optimum corrected by 64%. "Heteroscedastic surrogates address a case we do not cover" while the instrument and saturating-cap arms are heteroscedastic by construction. "Recovers 29–38%" not derivable from Table 7.
