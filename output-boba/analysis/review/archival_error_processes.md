# Error processes estimated from the three archival studies

Generated by `scripts/estimate_archival_error_processes.py`. Intervals are participant bootstraps (2000 resamples of participants with replacement) and are conditional on the leave-one-participant-out (LOPO) design model. "paper x" is the value the estimator would read if the paper's process held at the measured noise level. For the lag-1 rows it is the estimator's simulated reading under AR(1) with rho = 0.8 at these session lengths, and "white noise" is its reading with no serial dependence. A parenthesised flag marks a quantity that the design does not identify, or identifies only weakly (the `identification` column of the CSV). The full table is `archival_error_processes.csv`, and the counts and checks are in `archival_error_processes.json`.

## Reproduction of the paper's anchor, with intervals

- **ehmi**: the nugget is 0.2692 rating points (stored 0.2692). sigma_f is 0.3647 in the manifest and 0.3647 recomputed. The ratio 0.738 equals the paper's 0.738. Its participant-bootstrap 95% interval is 0.74 [0.48, 0.97], from 123 close pairs.
- **opticarvis**: the nugget is 1.2535 rating points (stored 1.2535). sigma_f is 0.3737 in the manifest and 0.3737 recomputed. The ratio 3.354 equals the paper's 3.354. Its participant-bootstrap 95% interval is 3.35 [3.03, 3.58], from 181 close pairs.
- **provoice**: the nugget is 0.3039 rating points (stored 0.3039). sigma_f is 0.2984 in the manifest and 0.2984 recomputed. The ratio 1.019 equals the paper's 1.019. Its participant-bootstrap 95% interval is 1.02 [0.20, 1.56] (weak), from 16 close pairs.

The paper's range of 0.74-3.35 therefore has intervals of its own. The top of the range is an artefact of the opticarvis scale mixing described below.

## Two defects in the pipeline's composites

**Opticarvis mixes two rating scales.** 40 of the 586 deduplicated rows (Run [0], groups ['GroupB', 'GroupE']) log the raw instrument values, on the 1-5, 1-7 and -3..3 scales. Every other row logs the value the optimiser saw, rescaled to [-1, 1] as (x - midpoint) / half-range. Their composite means are 4.10 and 0.57. 22 raw-scale rows sit on the GroupE standard design, which every GroupE participant rated. The pipeline averages both scales into one composite, so that design is a spike of about 4 on a surface that otherwise lies between -1 and 1. The spike sets the oracle's optimum (y_opt = 5.36, above the normalised maximum of 1, hence opt_z = 13.4), carries its held-out R^2, and inflates the nugget.

- **Pipeline composite.** The nugget is 3.35 [3.03, 3.58] sigma_f, and 0.44 [0.32, 0.56] without the close pairs that involve a raw-scale row.
- **Scale-consistent rows.** The nugget is 0.67 [0.49, 0.85] in this basis's own sigma_f (0.2577), and 0.46 [0.34, 0.59] in the paper's sigma_f.
- **Held-out (LOPO) R^2 of the pipeline's gradient-boosting model.** It is 0.76 [0.68, 0.83] on the pipeline composite and -0.21 [-0.50, -0.04] on the scale-consistent rows, where no family tried reaches zero. The pipeline's own group-CV R^2 is 0.518. The opticarvis oracle's apparent fidelity comes from the spike.

**Provoice enters Predictability with the wrong sign.** The logged Predictability is lower-is-better. The study's own R scripts invert it (6 - x) "because predictability is maximised", its Pareto rows average 1.3 against 2.4 for the rest, and it correlates -0.70 with Usefulness and +0.46 with Mental Demand. `datasets.json` enters it as +Predictability, so the pipeline composite partly cancels two constructs that agree. The sign-corrected composite ['-Predictability', 'Percieved Usefulness', '-Mental Demand'] has sigma_f 0.4486, against 0.2984. Its nugget is 0.60 [0.00, 0.90] (weak) sigma_f, and its LOPO R^2 is 0.08 [-0.04, 0.15], against 0.09 [-0.04, 0.16] for the pipeline composite.

## Verdict against the simulation's assumptions

- **Noise anchor.** ehmi 0.74 [0.48, 0.97]; opticarvis 0.67 [0.49, 0.85]; provoice 1.02 [0.20, 1.56]; provoice (sign-corrected) 0.60 [0.00, 0.90] sigma_f. The paper's 3.35 for opticarvis is 3.35 [3.03, 3.58] on the mixed-scale composite, and 0.46 [0.34, 0.59] on the scale-consistent rows in the paper's sigma_f. The provoice value rests on 16 pairs. On consistent composites, rating noise is roughly 0.5-1 sigma_f, not 0.74-3.35. If errors were serially dependent the nugget would understate it, because most of its pairs are consecutive trials.
- **AR(1), rho = 0.8: outside.** The pooled, bias-corrected lag-1 of the residuals is ehmi 0.44 [0.27, 0.64], opticarvis 0.27 [0.11, 0.43], provoice 0.44 [0.26, 0.62], provoice (sign-corrected) 0.61 [0.38, 0.79]. The same estimator reads 0.81-0.82 under the paper's AR(1), and 4 of 4 intervals exclude that. After each session's linear trend is removed, the lag-1 is near the white-noise reading (0.10 vs -0.10, -0.22 vs -0.19, -0.03 vs -0.14, 0.05 vs -0.14), so the correlation is mostly session-specific trend. If the rest of the residual were white, an AR(1) with rho = 0.8 could carry at most 62/15/50/58% of the residual variance (upper 95% bounds). Model-free, ehmi's close designs differ more on consecutive trials than far apart (semivariance ratio 1.65 [0.41, 8.40], against 0.31 under AR(1)), the opposite of carry-over. Opticarvis's ratio is uninformative, and provoice has 2 long-lag close pairs. An AR(1) with rho = 0.8 as the whole error process is outside the data. A minority serially-correlated component is not excluded.
- **DRIFT, a common linear ramp to +sigma_e: not identified, except in provoice.** Residual drift reverses sign when the model's predicted search progress is removed in full instead of at the fitted b: ehmi 1.21 [0.40, 1.61] vs -0.54 [-1.31, -0.13], and provoice 0.99 [-0.96, 3.09] vs -0.71 [-2.67, 0.97] sigma_f per session. The one identified estimate is provoice's counterbalanced crossover: 1.94 [1.24, 2.68] sigma_f per session on the pipeline composite (paper 1.02; 1.91 [1.03, 8.26] noise SDs against 1) and 0.74 [-0.36, 1.63] on the sign-corrected one (paper 0.60). It is construct-specific. In logged rating points per session, Mental Demand changes by -1.52 [-2.25, -0.84] (it eases), Predictability by 0.37 [-0.02, 0.96] (lower is better, so it worsens, borderline), and Usefulness by -0.15 [-0.72, 0.26]. Session slopes take both signs (ehmi 5 [0, 16]%, opticarvis 8 [2, 15]%, provoice 24 [11, 42]%, provoice (sign-corrected) 16 [0, 26]% significantly negative), and the between-session SD of drift is 1.59 [0.89, 2.15], 1.06 [0.61, 1.36], 4.00 [3.04, 4.69], 3.75 [2.59, 4.73] sigma_f. That points to rater-specific drift rather than one common positive ramp, though search progress the model misses can contribute. Provoice's drift magnitude is about one to two noise SDs per session, within reach of the paper's, but not its shape.
- **BIAS, a constant per-rater offset: inside, as a between-rater spread.** The offset SD over the within SD (BIAS = 1) is ehmi 0.97 [0.73, 1.13], opticarvis 1.42 [1.03, 1.82], provoice 0.62 [0.39, 0.79], provoice (sign-corrected) 0.71 [0.51, 0.82]. The model-free variance share on the fixed shared designs is 0.61 [0.45, 0.72], 0.77 [0.41, 0.90], 0.35 [0.14, 0.52], 0.44 [0.17, 0.59], with 0.5 inside 4 of 4 intervals. Raters differ by about one noise SD in level, so one rater offset by sigma_e is a plausible draw. Whether every rater is shifted the same way is unidentifiable.
- **Homoscedastic gaussian noise: outside.** The residual SD in the top prediction tercile relative to the bottom one is ehmi 0.45 [0.30, 0.62], opticarvis 0.61 [0.47, 0.85], provoice 0.71 [0.57, 0.84], provoice (sign-corrected) 0.74 [0.61, 0.90], with 1 outside 4 of 4 intervals. The model-free close-pair slope agrees in sign, significantly in ehmi (-0.45 [-1.66, -0.04]). High-rated designs are rated more consistently, as a ceiling compresses them. The excess kurtosis is 5.2 [2.8, 7.8], 9.1 [3.3, 15.1], 4.0 [1.6, 7.1], 2.7 [0.9, 5.9], and 2.4 [1.1, 4.0]%, 1.8 [0.8, 3.0]%, 1.7 [0.8, 2.6]%, 1.7 [0.9, 2.6]% of residuals lie beyond 3 SD, against 0.3% under a gaussian.
- **Saturation: the unbounded main arms are unrealistic.** At least one construct is at its best-end bound in ehmi 83 [74, 91]%, opticarvis 55 [43, 66]%, provoice 61 [47, 73]%, provoice (sign-corrected) 63 [48, 77]% of ratings, and in 88 [78, 96]%, 60 [47, 72]%, 73 [58, 86]%, 76 [61, 89]% in the last third of a session. The capped-scale arm, not the uncapped main sweep, is the realistic case for the top of the landscape.

## What the data can and cannot identify

- **Search progress confounds trial order.** Later designs are better, so a naive rating-versus-trial slope mixes drift with progress. A LOPO design model removes progress only as well as it predicts held-out participants. Its R^2 is 0.23 (ehmi/pipeline), -0.21 (opticarvis/scale_consistent), 0.09 (provoice/pipeline), 0.08 (provoice/sign_corrected). Within a session, trial and predicted quality rise together, so a weak model cannot divide the trend between them. The model-based drift moves from the fitted b to b = 1 as more of the model's progress is removed, and the two differ in sign for ehmi/pipeline, provoice/pipeline, provoice/sign_corrected. Model-based drift is therefore flagged weak or not identified.
- **Fixed sampling sequences.** ehmi (36 of 37 participants) and provoice show every participant the same initial designs in the same order. Within a session, the sampling phase cannot separate drift from design. The LOPO prediction of those rows is the other participants' mean at the same position, which absorbs any drift common to all participants.
- **The model-free estimates.** Provoice's counterbalanced crossover (the same design rated in the first and in the second condition of one sitting) is free of both confounds, and it is the one drift estimate the design identifies. It assumes no carryover between conditions. Close-design-pair drift is biased low: a pair exists because the optimiser returned near an earlier design, which it does when that design was rated well, so the earlier rating is selected upward. It rests on 33 (ehmi/pipeline), 108 (opticarvis/scale_consistent), 2 (provoice/pipeline), 2 (provoice/sign_corrected) close pairs at lag >= 4.
- **Serial dependence.** The lag-1 correlation of residuals is not a clean estimate of a rater's serial dependence. Design-model misfit that consecutive close designs share inflates it, and session-to-session differences in drift inflate it too. Misfit that is white along the trajectory dilutes it. Removing each session's linear trend deals with the drift, and the near/far split checks the shared misfit. The share row converts the detrended lag-1 into the largest share of residual variance an AR(1) with rho = 0.8 could carry. The close-pair semivariogram needs no model: under AR(1), close designs rated on consecutive trials would differ far less than close designs rated far apart, unless the design difference within a close pair exceeds the noise. The nugget's close pairs are mostly consecutive trials, so if errors were AR(1) the nugget would understate the noise (the attenuation row).
- **A constant offset shared by every rater cannot be identified**, because nothing measures a design's value except the ratings. What can be identified is how much raters differ from one another: the random-intercept share. Because each session is personalised, that share also absorbs design-model misfit in each participant's region of the design space. The shared-design row is its model-free check, on the fixed sampling designs.
- **Short sessions.** Slopes and lag-1 use sessions of at least 6 trials. At 10-20 trials, a per-session slope test has little power even under the paper's exact DRIFT, which is the paper value shown in that row. The test is also anti-conservative if residuals are autocorrelated.
