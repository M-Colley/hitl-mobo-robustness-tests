## 6. What the tables say about the three points

**(i) The headline is not an artefact of multimodal test functions.** At 1 SD from the first rating the deployed cost is +32.7% [+29, +37] of opt_z on the six laws and +27.8% [+24, +32] on the thirteen classical functions (all 20: +29.7% [+27, +33]; laws minus classical +4.8% [-1, +11]). The search cost is larger on the laws (+19.7% vs +11.6%), and the selection share is the same (+40% [+30, +49] vs +42% [+30, +56]). With error from trial 21 the laws lose less in total but a larger share of it is selection (section 2).

**(ii) Per landscape.** Every one of the twenty landscapes has a deployed cost above zero at 1 SD from trial 1 (median 29.0%, range 18.3% to 46.6%), so the pooled mean describes a consistent effect. The selection share is heterogeneous (range 4% to 85%; lowest on shekel, ackley, hartmann_6, where the search itself fails most), so a pooled share needs the per-landscape figure beside it. `per_landscape_headline.csv` is the tidy table for that figure.

**(iii) / question 6: yes, on the smooth landscapes pm beats the single best rating, and the reviewer's misspecification explanation is largely right for gaussian error.** Under gaussian error pm recovers +11.3% [+9, +15] of the deployed cost on the six laws at 1 SD from trial 1 (6 of 6 better) and +10.8% [+4, +17] at 5 SD (5 of 6), against +1.2% [-5, +7] and +1.3% [-4, +6] on the classical functions. The secondary split puts the line at smoothness, not at 'law': pooled over >= 0.25 SD, pm recovers +11.5% [+6, +16] on the laws, +9.2% [+2, +17] on the three smooth classical functions and -7.4% [-14, -1] on the ten rugged ones. With six landscapes the smallest attainable two-sided Wilcoxon p is 0.031, which the 1 SD cells reach.

The GP diagnostics say why.

- The noise estimate is NOT what separates the groups: at n = 50 the fitted noise SD is 0.84 [0.81, 0.87] of the injected SD on the laws and 0.85 [0.81, 0.89] on the classical functions: largely recovered in both (short by 16% and 15%), as the reviewer says.
- The posterior mean does shrink the ratings everywhere (RMSE against the truth at the visited designs is 0.52 of the raw ratings' on the laws, 0.67 on the classical, 0.71 on the rugged ten), but less where the fit is worse, and its rank advantage over the ratings among the visited designs is +0.20 (rank correlation) on the laws against +0.08 on the classical functions.
- Misspecification is visible with NO error at all. On clean runs best_observed is the best visited design, so pm's price there is pure surrogate error: 0.0003 of opt_z on the laws, 0.0029 on the smooth classical three and 0.0295 on the rugged ten. The clean refits agree: RMSE of the posterior mean against the exact values it was fitted to is 0.012 landscape SDs on the laws against 0.185 on the rugged ten; the GP explains unmodelled roughness as noise (fitted 'noise' SD 0.03 on the laws, 0.03 on the smooth classical, 0.30 on the rugged ten, where the true noise is zero); lengthscales are 0.85 against 0.34 box widths; held-out R^2 at fresh Sobol points is 0.67 against -0.24.
- Across landscapes fit quality predicts pm's gain: with the LOO fraction of achievable predictive gain, rho = +0.62 (p = 0.004, n = 20) over all 20 and rho = +0.85 (p = 0.000, n = 13) within the classical functions; with the shrinkage ratio rho = -0.71 (p = 0.000, n = 20). Within a landscape, run-to-run variation in fit does not predict the run's outcome (rho = +0.07 (p = 0.175, n = 400)), so fit acts as a property of the landscape, and a per-run fit diagnostic would not tell a practitioner when to trust pm.
- The corrupted-posterior explanation survives where the LIKELIHOOD is wrong. Under drift and bias, which the GP's i.i.d. noise model cannot represent, pm loses even on the laws late in a study: at 5 SD from trial 21, -15.9% [-22, -9] under drift and -9.6% [-22, +2] under bias (under ar1 +4.1% [-2, +13]). Pooled over the four processes the rugged ten give -9.2% [-15, -4].
- The paper's pooled selection-loss figure for pm (every cell pooled) reproduces here as -8.7% [-17, -1] over all 20; it splits into +9.0% [+2, +15] on the laws and -15.7% [-28, -6] on the classical functions.

So the sentence 'the posterior ... inherits the corrupted ratings' is too broad. On smooth landscapes under i.i.d. error the posterior mean shrinks the noise and pm beats the single best rating. pm's pooled loss comes from two sources: surrogate misspecification on the rugged classical functions, which already costs pm a price on clean runs, and likelihood misspecification under drift and bias, which costs it even on smooth landscapes once the error starts late. The cautious rules are comparable to pm on the laws (gaussian, >= 0.25 SD pooled: lcb1 +13.8%, lcb2 +13.9%, pm +11.5%) and hold up on the rugged ten, where pm does not (lcb2 +2.5%, pm -7.4%).
